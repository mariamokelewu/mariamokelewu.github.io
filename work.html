Selected Work

Evidence, context and judgement.

A selection of examples from my work. Each begins with a practical question and turns on understanding the context before reaching a conclusion.

Cases & decisions

These examples illustrate how I approach analysis, risk, regulation, and commercial judgement. They are not intended to represent the full scope of my responsibilities in each role.

HMRC · Risk Assessment

When unusual activity needs context

A large international shipping business presented a complex reporting picture. I brought together fragmented records, examined the company's UK operations and relevant reporting arrangements, and assessed the evidence before concluding that the business's activities in the UK were legitimate.

RealJoy Schools · Commercial Review

When the real cost isn't in the first payment

The school wanted to improve its IT infrastructure and establish a longer-term relationship with an IT provider. The initial proposal appeared to meet what had been requested.

However, the commercial picture was different. Association fees, potential repair and replacement costs, ongoing internet charges and a further payment after two years would have added costs beyond the initial arrangement.

I raised these concerns with management and, when the arrangement was no longer considered suitable, looked for alternative providers.

A contract can look reasonable when each term is considered separately, while the relationship it creates over time tells a different story.

Nutti · Commercial Operations

Knowing the product before making the decision

While working with international manufacturers, I challenged a proposed material choice, looking at its composition, durability, elasticity and breathability, as well as comparable products and market expectations. I discussed my findings with the founder, sharing the evidence behind my research and my perspective on the options before he made the final choice of material.

HMRC · Risk Assessment

When the evidence changes the picture

A pilot operating international flights between the UK and France submitted a fuel refund claim.

A mismatch between the claim and the receipts provided raised the question of whether the discrepancy was due to an error or something more serious.

I reviewed additional evidence and considered the potential causes before concluding that the mismatch was due to an error rather than an attempt to make a fraudulent claim.

The examples are specific. The questions travel.
